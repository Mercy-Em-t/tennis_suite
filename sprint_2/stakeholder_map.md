# Stakeholder & Persona Definition

## Primary Stakeholders
- **Users (Players)**: The core user base interacting with the matchmaking (LFG Drafter), progression systems, and scoring engines. They want fair competition and seamless organization.
- **Customers (Club Owners / Facility Managers / Tournament Directors)**: The paying entities that purchase Tennis Suite to manage their courts, finances, and events. They want reduced overhead and increased court utilization.
- **Owners**: The Tennis Suite development, engineering, and business team. They are focused on system scalability, revenue growth, and platform stability.
- **Software Agents/Bots**: Internal autonomous actors such as the Cinematic AI (generating commentary) and LFG matchmaking algorithms (balancing teams).

## Secondary Stakeholders
- **Inspectors/Referees**: End-users who utilize the Referee dashboard and dispute resolution tools to maintain match integrity.
- **Broadcasters**: Users who consume Server-Sent Events (SSE) feeds to drive real-time overlays for streaming.

## Other Stakeholders
- **Market Researchers / Analysts**: Interested in aggregated player progression data, court usage trends, and demographic engagement.
- **Lawyers / Industry Standards**: Entities ensuring compliance with USTA/ITF rules, as well as data privacy laws (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) for player data.
- **Experts on Adjacent Systems**: Integrators and IT staff who connect Tennis Suite with existing club Point of Sale (POS) and legacy scheduling systems.

## Negative Stakeholders
- **Disfavored Users**: Smurfs (highly skilled players on new accounts), griefers, or bad actors who intentionally dispute scores or exploit the matchmaking system. The system addresses these via the Outlier Detection and Dispute Resolution engines.
